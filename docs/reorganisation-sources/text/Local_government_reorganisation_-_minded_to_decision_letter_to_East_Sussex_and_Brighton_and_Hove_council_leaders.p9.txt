I recognise that this is a finely balanced judgement and that the risks will require careful management through the transition and governance arrangements described above. Nevertheless, I judge that the marginal sustainability gain, together with the further potential to strengthen the tax base through economic and housing growth and to reduce costs through more efficient public services, outweighs the additional short-term costs and transition risks. Taken together, these financial sustainability and public service benefits provide the strong justification required for the boundary change. Conclusion For the reasons set out above and considering the options’ performance against the criteria in the round, I am currently minded to proceed with a modified two unitary option. The factors that I currently regard as weighing particularly strongly are the functional economic, travel and service links between Brighton and Hove and the four transferred wards (criterion 1); the opportunity to bring planning, housing, regeneration, transport and local public services within one authority across that coastal area (criteria 1 and 3); and the broader population and planning footprint available to Brighton and Hove (criterion 2). I have weighed those advantages against the more complete evidence on efficiencies and lower implementation risk of the original two unitary option, the additional cost and ward-level disaggregation required by the modification, the effects on residual East Sussex, which I judge to remain of sufficient scale and resilience to absorb the boundary change, representations made by local partners, and the strength of local representation to the modified two unitary option. I have given greater weight to the modified option’s better fit with the coastal communities’ functional urban area and geography and its wider tax and planning base, which offer greater long-term potential for coordinated growth, housing and local services. I have not selected the five unitary proposal because it does not meet criteria 2, 3 and 4. It would create less resilient councils, carry substantially greater implementation and service risks, and was developed without support from other councils in the area. Status of this provisional view This letter does not constitute a final decision to implement this or any other proposal. The conclusions set out above represent my current provisional view, reached following consideration of the relevant material. Before reaching a final decision, I will consider all representations received in response to this letter together with any other relevant material. I remain open to reaching a different conclusion following that consideration, including modifying the provisionally preferred proposal further, deciding to implement a different proposal, or deciding not to implement any proposal. Invitation for representations Relevant councils and other interested parties will be able to make representations on the provisional conclusions and reasoning set out in this letter. This representation period will be for a minimum of three weeks. I will confirm the exact timings shortly. Representations should, where possible, address the provisional conclusions and any specific requests for information identified in this letter. I am grateful for the time already given to prepare and submit proposals, views and supporting evidence. There is no need to reproduce material submitted previously or repeat points that have already been made: that material will remain available to me when reaching the final decision. 9
