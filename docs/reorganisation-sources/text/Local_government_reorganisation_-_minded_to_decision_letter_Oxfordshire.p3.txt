Although the proposed Greater Oxford council would not encompass the whole functional urban area or fully resolve Oxford’s underbounding, we consider that it would represent a material improvement on the existing geography and may be a more effective approach to addressing housing challenges around Oxford, which could in turn better support economic growth across Oxfordshire. We have also considered the other two proposed authorities under the three unitary option in their own right. The proposed Northern Oxfordshire council would bring together most of Cherwell and West Oxfordshire. It would include the established market towns such as Banbury, Bicester and Witney, alongside surrounding rural communities with distinct identities. This would provide an authority focused on the particular housing, economic, transport and service needs of those towns and their surrounding areas. It would contain significant employment centres, planned housing growth and strategic road and rail connections. The proposed Ridgeway council would bring together the remaining parts of South Oxfordshire and Vale of White Horse with West Berkshire. It would include a network of towns, employment centres and more dispersed communities across southern Oxfordshire and West Berkshire, connected in part through Science Vale and the A34 and M4 corridors. In our judgment, the evidence submitted provides a credible basis for treating the proposed Northern Oxfordshire council and Ridgeway council as coherent geographies. In Northern Oxfordshire, the pattern of market towns and surrounding rural communities provides a basis for coordinating growth, infrastructure and services across the proposed authority. In Ridgeway, there are relevant connections between settlements, employment centres and strategic transport corridors, although these relationships vary across the area and do not all operate at the same geographical scale. We have weighed these limitations against the evidence of common characteristics and practical connections within each area. On balance, we consider that each could provide a sufficiently coherent economic area and a sensible geography for housing, infrastructure, and local government with an appropriate tax base. Turning to the other proposals, which meet criterion 1, the one unitary proposal would retain the existing Oxfordshire boundary and provide a single strategic framework for housing, infrastructure, transport and economic development. It offers the greatest economies of scale and simplicity and the most balanced geography. The proposal states that a countywide authority could plan for Oxford’s growth alongside the needs of the wider county, coordinate housing and infrastructure across existing district boundaries and maintain local influence through area-based arrangements. Departmental analysis also indicates that the one unitary proposal aligns well with the identified Oxford functional urban area. We recognise these advantages. However, Oxford’s urban, housing and infrastructure pressures would need to be balanced against the priorities of other towns and rural communities across the county. A single countywide geography may not be as able to provide dedicated leadership for Oxford and its adjoining communities or make the wider Oxford area the central focus of the new authority. The three unitary option sets out that competing urban and rural priorities have previously made it difficult to agree the location and scale of growth needed around Oxford. On balance, we place greater weight on the more focused geography offered by the proposed Greater Oxford Council under the three unitary option.
