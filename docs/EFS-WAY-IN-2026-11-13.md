Round two prompt 4 from UK Council Tax's `docs/CODEX-PROMPTS-2026-11-12.md`.
Based on UK Councils main `9c2b356`, which includes Estate Kit 0.5.0.

## Behaviour

The finder above the contents accepts an authority name, county or region.
It reuses the picker's `matchesCouncil`, `matchingRegion` and `normalise`
helpers, and the existing `rankCouncils` helper. Billing council metadata
comes from `englishRows`; EFS membership joins by the held GSS code.
The six authorities without a profile remain findable and retain their types.
County geography uses the registry's exact parent GSS join. No geography is
inferred for the former district or police bodies.

Choosing an authority opens its council block, scrolls to it and focuses its
heading. All existing authority, heading and council-year anchors remain.
Load, hash change and repeated clicks reveal the target. Same-page clicks
retain the existing fragment in browser history without native fragment
navigation taking focus back from the heading. Modified clicks retain their
normal browser behaviour.

Billing councils absent from the EFS list receive a "Not listed on this page"
message and their profile link. This is a statement about the page's coverage,
not a claim that the council has received no support. Searches remain in the
browser and are neither stored nor sent anywhere.

Every council block is a native closed details element. Its summary names the
authority and the source page years on which it appears, rather than claiming
that those are the years to which every amount relates. The yearly tables
and the alphabetical index remain open. Without JavaScript, the finder is
hidden and every council block can be opened using its summary.

## Small design choices

The finder lists up to eight results and asks the reader to narrow longer
matches. Exact names are first, followed by the existing name ranking and
alphabetical order. Results show the authority type and held region so county
and police bodies are not mistaken for billing councils. The original full
alphabetical index is retained as a direct route and as a scripts-off aid.

No obsolete assertion needed replacement. The existing EFS source and browser
tests are unchanged. New browser coverage also runs under actual production
security headers.
